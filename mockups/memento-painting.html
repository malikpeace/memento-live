The painting, four stages of one life

Same person, same rules, four moments in time. Green is a day you acted, cyan is a day you wrote, white is a record. The marks adapt to their count: when there are five, each is an ember with a halo. As the record grows they shrink and gather into weather. Nothing is random: position comes from the date, size from the count, weight from what the day was.

This is the honest test of the sparse canvas. Judge stage two hardest.

The adaptive rule, plainly: mark size scales with rarity, so five marks are five jewels and five hundred are weather. Early days get halos and long tails because each one is an event; later marks get density instead. The composition is anchored the same way the card's blobs already are, seeded by the name, so the painting is theirs from stroke one and only ever gains. Missed days paint nothing, and unpainted canvas is not a wrong mark, it is room. This lives INSIDE the card, behind the pristine face, by Malik's call, August 8.
