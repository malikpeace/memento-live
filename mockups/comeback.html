Comeback, 5 ways

You have been gone six days. This is the first screen when you open the app again. Right now nothing special happens, which is the single biggest retention hole in Memento.

01No speech

Six days.

Send Marcus the message you never sent.

What it is. Two words about the absence, then a small move. No pep talk.
Good. Cheap to build and the smaller move is what actually gets someone going again. Currently returning gets you nothing at all.
Bad. Some people want an acknowledgement of the break. This gives them almost nothing to hold onto.
Seen in Duolingo's welcome-back lesson, Headspace's restart, Strava's return prompts.

02What is still true

Nothing you built went away.

Streakgone

One number reset. The other three did not.

What it is. A list of exactly what survived the break, including the streak that did not.
Good. Attacks the real reason people quit after a lapse: believing the whole thing is ruined.
Bad. It is a screen of numbers at an emotional moment, and showing 'streak: gone' in writing may land as a punishment.
Seen in Duolingo's streak repair, Apple Fitness' award history, Strava's totals.

03The gap, drawn

Jul 18

Pick the thread back up

The gap stays in your trail. It is part of it.

What it is. The gap drawn permanently into the timeline, labelled with how long it was.
Good. Honest instead of forgiving, and it refuses to call it a restart. The version most likely to earn respect.
Bad. A permanent visible scar. For some people that is motivating and for others it is a reason to delete the app.
Seen in GitHub's contribution gaps, Strava's training log, Apple Health's missing days.

04Your own last words

The last thing you wrote, six days ago

"I keep building instead of talking to people."

Still true?

What it is. You are met by the last thing you wrote before the break.
Good. The app says nothing. Your own voice lands harder than any coaching line, and it costs nothing once daily writing exists.
Bad. Depends completely on there being something written, and the last entry might be bleak enough to push someone further away.
Seen in Day One's on-this-day, Apple Photos' memories, Timehop.

05The cold card

It went grey on July 20.

One finished day brings the colour back.

What it is. The card went grey while you were away. One finished day brings it back.
Good. The consequence is visual and already happened, so nothing has to lecture you. Fits the object-first premise perfectly.
Bad. Needs the card cooling logic built first, and a punished-looking card is a real risk of making returning feel worse.
Seen in Finch's pet drooping, Forest's withered trees, Duolingo's cracked flame.
