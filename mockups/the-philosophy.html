The philosophy of Memento

The whole machine on one page. Collapsed, it reads as the flow. Tap any step to open it; tap "go deeper" inside for the edge cases. Built to be challenged.

The principle everything hangs on. A goal is an outcome you do not control, reached through behaviors you do. Memento translates one into the other, then shows whichever is honest that day.

The machine asks two questions, in order: 1. What would count? 2. What do you control? Then it makes enough volume of the controllable that the outcome becomes unreasonable to miss. Volume negates luck.

Their words enter at the top. A finished goal loops back.

1 Their words Clarity "I want to keep bees." Nothing is required of the sentence.

People arrive with goals in whatever shape their life produced them: a number, a dream, a fear, a vow. The machine never demands a format, because the first moment of the app must not feel like a form.

Why this matters: every downstream step exists so that ANY sentence a person types lands somewhere sensible. The burden of structure is on Memento, never on them.

2 The signature Clarity Clarity sharpens the sentence into a star and keeps exactly three things.

The discovery conversation ends with a signed statement. Three things are kept, nothing more:

KeptWhy
The goaltheir words, sharpened, signed
The whyin THEIR words, lightly copyedited: spelling and clarity fixed, never rewritten. A why written for you moves nothing.
The horizonasked, not just parsed: how long is this? Maintenance with no end? It becomes the deadline flag.
The anti-visionwhat it costs them if this stays neglected. The stick beside the carrot.

The identity line was cut: it read as corny, and three is already the full load-bearing set.

The boundary test: if it cannot be the point of a year of your life, it is an action, not a star.
Go deeper: one star at a time

Memento holds ONE star, on purpose; that is the thesis of the app. The answer to "but I have a fitness goal AND a business goal" is: one star, and everything else lives underneath it as rules and maintenance. An app that lets you hold five goals is a list app, and lists do not change anyone.

Go deeper: star vs rule

Sentences like "don't decide anything about the marriage until January" classify fine (maintenance, hold-until) and have real progress (time held). But they are seasonal RULES, not stars, so they belong as Hold actions under a larger goal. The classifier absorbs them so nothing breaks; Clarity's job is to steer the person to a star worth chasing and quietly file the rules underneath it.

Targets are optional but heavily encouraged. Forcing a number is too strict, but a number makes the goal observable and unlocks the derivation chain below: "lose 40 lbs in 6 months" can become a calorie line; "lose some weight" can only become "eat less", which is not a plan. Clarity nudges hard for a number wherever one is possible; only Open goals are exempt.

How it is detected: one field on the Clarity synthesis. The AI that just wrote the anti-vision names the type. Never a bigger regex; a regex cannot see that "become debt free" is quantity-down.

Go deeper: the deadline flag

A deadline is a modifier on any type, not a type itself, and it comes in two forms: a date ("by June 14") or a bounding event of unknown timing ("before he dies", "before the baby comes").

  • On most types it means achieve-by.
  • On maintenance it means hold-until: reaching the date with the line unbroken IS success, and the goal completes (a no-buy year).
  • On conditional goals it can be relative to a trigger ("within 90 days of X").
Go deeper: the two tiebreak rules
  • The vow test (frequency vs maintenance): if one miss breaks the goal in the holder's mind ("never", "every single day"), it is maintenance. If a tolerant rate is the point, it is frequency.
  • Derived numbers count: a ratio, a proportion, or a rank is a legal quantity. "More time creating than consuming" is quantity-up of a ratio. "Be #1 in my market" is quantity-down of rank.
Go deeper: compound, sequenced, conditional
  • Compound: two goals wearing one sentence ("get fit and start a business"). Decompose into two.
  • Sequenced: the goal changes type over its life ("quit smoking" is quantity-down to zero, then maintenance forever).
  • Conditional: dormant until a trigger fires ("if the biopsy is malignant..."). Takes the type of its consequent, probabilistically.

Milestones usually hide an engine: "pass the boards" runs on hours studied. Discovery surfaces the engine; the type stays milestone.

Go deeper: the stress test receipt

Five adversaries invented 179 goals across niche crafts, inner life, social and status, weird logical structures, and athletic edges. 25 claimed the taxonomy broke. A judge attacked every claim; 3 survived, and all three were wording gaps in the deadline flag, now folded in. Nothing defeated the six types. If a real goal ever resists, bring the goal, do not re-litigate the categories.

Still open

  • Whether classification is shown to the user or stays invisible machinery.
4 Decompose: what do you control? Action The pivot. Outcomes are not controllable; inputs are. Find the inputs.

The scale is not controllable; eating under 2,500 is. Getting hired is not; ten applications a week is. Bees are not; building the brood box this weekend is. The AI names the input behaviors that cause the outcome, because pointing daily effort at an outcome produces guilt, and pointing it at inputs produces volume.

Milestone goals also get acts: becoming a beekeeper is learn, equip, install, tend, and the app should know which act you are in.

When a target and a deadline both exist, the rate is computed, not guessed: 40 lbs in 6 months is a 700-800 calorie daily deficit, which is "eat 2,000-2,400 a day". Memento shows its arithmetic so the person can confirm or correct it. And if the math demands an unsafe or unrealistic rate, Memento says so plainly and offers the honest timeline. An app willing to tell you your deadline is a lie is an app you can trust with the deadline.

A perfect action is controllable, checkable tonight, causal, and sized to today.
Go deeper: why this is the whole game

This is the volume-negates-luck step. If the inputs are truly causal and the volume is sustained, the outcome stops being a hope and becomes a consequence. Every display decision downstream follows the same ethic: lead with what they control, keep the outcome visible but quiet, because a noisy outcome week reads as a failure they did not commit.

5 Actions: verb, size, cadence Action Every action carries three properties, and each does a different job.

The verb decides what "done" means, and therefore what button the action gets:

Size is the five existing tiers, tiny to extreme, so a bad day still has a move that fits it.

Cadence is how often the action exists at all. Most are daily. A Check can be weekly. Some are seasonal. The gym at 3x a week is a cadence, and a schedule the user builds is a cadence too.

Go deeper: the 2pm lie

Today every action renders with a Mark Complete button. For a rule like "under 2,500 calories" that button at 2pm is a lie: a rule cannot be completed in the afternoon, it can only survive until midnight. Holds are the most common action type for Change and Keep goals, which is a huge share of real goals, and they need the evening confirm to be honest. This is the single biggest missing mechanic in the app.

Still open

  • Who sets cadence: the AI proposes and the user edits, or user-built schedules from scratch.
  • The celebration moment when a goal completes (Malik builds this).
6 The days: adherence, not attendance Consistency Consistency means you kept your own schedule, never that you appeared daily.

If the plan is 3x a week, then 3 of 3 is a perfect week, not a broken streak. Consistency is measured against the person's own cadence, so seasonal, weekly, and every-other-day goals never read as failure on their quiet days.

The grace bank already gestures at this; cadence makes it principled. The streak becomes "weeks you kept your word to yourself," which can survive a vacation and cannot be gamed by empty daily check-ins.

Go deeper: when the days do not move the number

The machine is not feed-forward only. On a cadence, Memento compares the expected rate against the actual rate. If the person is keeping their schedule and the number still is not moving, the inputs were not causal or the volume is too low, and the decomposition gets re-derived. Doing the actions and getting nothing is the moment most apps go silent; it is the moment a coach speaks up.

Go deeper: why streak-first design fails most people

A daily streak fits exactly one kind of user: someone whose goal genuinely demands daily identical effort. Everyone else either breaks it for structural reasons (shift workers, seasonal goals) and churns from guilt, or games it with token check-ins and the number stops meaning anything. Adherence keeps the emotional power of an unbroken line while pointing it at a promise the person actually made.

7 The record: evidence accumulates Consistency Every mark is kept in their own words. The record is the retention engine.

Every completed action, reflection, and milestone becomes a mark with the person's own words on it. Counters are abstract and easy to walk away from; "called the two churned users, Fri 31" is autobiography and cannot be rebuilt by starting over.

For open goals the record IS the progress. "Be a better father" gets no meter, ever. It gets evidence: the accumulating pile of things done in its name, which is the only honest display that type allows.

Go deeper: chapters

Because every replaced or finished star is kept, the record divides into chapters: the acts of what you were chasing and when. A two-year record stops being 700 rows and becomes a life with structure. This is the most underused data in the app.
