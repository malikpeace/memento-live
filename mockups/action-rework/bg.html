The room

Round two. Every phone carries the same specimen, the current statement screen: the three-line sentence, the deep work door, the hold. The only variable is what the black is made of. All eleven are STATIC, monochrome ink on the same near-black, with no blur and no radial anywhere: drafting sheets, instruments and pages, the workbench family without the grid itself. Rules, the one survivor of round one, is first and refined.

0Rules, refined

The survivor: wide-spaced ruled lines only, opened out and taken lighter, a page waiting for one line of writing.

1Crosshairs

Four registration marks at the corners, the way a drafting sheet is aligned. The centre stays clear.

2Margin

One vertical hairline down the left, a manuscript margin, and the sentence hangs off it.

3Baseline

Baselines only where the type actually sits: a type-specimen sheet, so the empty page stays empty.

4Ticks

A ruler's edge down the left, a tick every twelve pixels and a longer one every fifth: an instrument.

5Frame

A single hairline drawing-frame inset from the edge, corners notched open like a technical drawing border.

6Points

THE CHOSEN ROOM. A dot where the lines would have crossed, breathing: scattered dots fade in and out on slow uneven cycles. Now the whole gallery wears it.

7Column

Two hairlines set the measure of the text, and outside them the page steps a half value darker.

8Sections

Two faint separators cut the screen into its three working zones: the statement, the size, the commitment.

9Brushed

Vertical micro-striations at the edge of visibility: a machined face, directional and pitched, not grain.

10Ledger

Alternating row shading at about a one percent lightness delta: bookkeeping paper, order before pattern.
