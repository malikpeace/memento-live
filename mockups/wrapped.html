Tap the Memento: six ways to show the year

One person, one goal, one set of numbers, six layouts. Change the design, not the data, so you are judging the presentation.

The person: signed Get to 100 paying users on February 3. 186 days in, 62 users, showed up 141 days, longest run 23, best month June.

Each frame scrolls on its own. The Wrapped one swipes sideways.

The Wrapped

Spotify Wrapped, literally. Full-bleed panels you swipe sideways, one enormous number each.

Loudest. Zero scrolling: it is a deck, so it is the same three flicks on day 1 and day 900. The risk is it feels like a slideshow you have already watched.

The Dossier

One dense page. Everything visible at once, nothing hidden, cold and typographic.

The opposite of a slideshow. Nothing to swipe, nothing to wait for. Reads like a case file on your own goal. Risk: it is a wall of numbers, which is what you rejected before.

The Year in Squares

One bounded grid holds everything. Days you showed up light up inside it. It gets denser, never longer.

This is the memorial shape. The grid is a fixed object, so the page is the same height forever. Everything else is one line of text around it.

The Ledger

Month by month, one row each, a bar for the days you showed up.

Caps at twelve rows a year. Reads like a statement of effort. Honest about bad months instead of hiding them.

The Ascent

A single line from where you started to where you are going, with you on it.

One screen, no scroll at all. The most emotional of the set because it shows the gap, not the stats.

Two Numbers

Almost nothing. The distance you closed and the days you showed up. Everything else behind one tap.

The quietest. Closest to a memorial and the hardest to get bored of, because there is nothing to get bored of.
