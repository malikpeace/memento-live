Your picks

The 44 you marked, on their own, in the order the sets appear. Nothing you passed on is here. The full set of 125 is still at all mockups.

What your 44 have in common

You picked a tool that proposes and then gets out of the way, and rejected almost everything that displays, comforts or decorates. The rejections are as consistent as the picks.

1. You killed every dashboard

Of the dozen renders whose only job was showing numbers, you took zero. Not the scoreboard, not numbers-only, not this-week, not against-last-week, not bento, not three columns, not the clean list. On page two you picked one of fifteen: 08, the work page, the only one with steps and a notes field. On desktop you picked the four most work-dense of the ten. Both pages are for doing, not admiring.

2. Chronology beats metaphor

Four of your six proof trail picks (spine, receipt roll, transit line, raw log) are the same object at different densities: a dated vertical list. You rejected every abstract visualisation, tree rings, strata, constellation, blocks, momentum curve. The one shaped exception you kept is the ladder, which is goal-shaped rather than art-shaped.

3. Structure where you act, freedom where you reflect

In Action you took the brief, the two doors, the schedule and the swipe. In Reflect you took blank page, voice and the letter, and rejected both structured ones. That is not inconsistent, it is a rule: tell me what to do, do not tell me what to think.

4. The object carries the emotion, not the copy

For the comeback you picked no-speech and the cold card, and rejected all three that talk to you, including the reassuring one. You also picked every-card-you-have-had. The Memento holds state, consequence and history. Words do not get to.

5. Glass and geometry, no organics, no awards

Your six object picks are all precise and lit: card, orb, photograph, vial, band, shard. You rejected every organic form (stone, bloom, ember), every metal award form (coin, pin, ingot), the monolith and the lantern. The photograph is your one personal exception. This matches the existing design law exactly, you did not drift.

6. It has to work on day one

Your three share picks all render with zero history and zero photos, and you rejected both that need months of accumulation. Evidence 12 (locked slots) and 13 (receipts instead of photos) do the same job: they lower the bar for having something to show early.

7. Notifications yes, widgets no

You took all three notification renders and neither widget. That is also the buildable half, since push is already wired and widgets would need a native app.

8. Mori stays philosophical

You took the three life-scale ones and rejected both that tie it back to the goal. Mori is not a productivity lever, it is a separate room.

The product your picks describe

Morning notification with the actual move. Open it, choose between two moves or swipe to accept. The move sits alone on the home under your object. Tap in for the brief, or drop it into a time slot. Do the work on the page-two work surface with notes. At night, two taps, and tomorrow is already set. Writing lives in its own room, blank or spoken. History is a dated list plus a ladder toward the number. Go quiet and the object goes cold, and coming back is one small move with no lecture.

That is coherent. It is a decision engine with a keepsake attached, not a tracker.

Three things your picks collide on

Where does the writing come from?

You rejected Close the day 03, the one-line capture, but picked Proof trail 10, which is nothing but your own sentences. So the trail has to pull from Reflect, which makes Reflect load-bearing rather than optional. Blank page and voice were the right picks for exactly that reason, but it means Reflect has to actually get used or the trail is empty.

Evidence 01 and 13 fight each other visually

A mosaic wants beautiful photographs. Receipts are ugly screenshots of payments and messages. Both are good ideas and they cannot share one grid. Either receipts get their own surface, or the wall has to be designed to hold ugly things gracefully.

Action 01, 11, 12, 14 and 15 cannot all be one screen

They are not competing though, they are five moments in one flow: propose (11 or 15), commit (14), the move alone (01), expand for detail (12). Pick 11 or 15 for the proposal. They are the same decision in two interaction styles.

The risk worth naming

You systematically rejected the retention mechanics. No safety floor on bad days (Action 04), no partial credit (Action 06), no "here is what survived" on return (Comeback 02), no vault (Page two more 05). Every one of those existed to catch someone who is slipping. Your picks optimise beautifully for the person who is already showing up and give almost nothing to the person drifting away on day nine. That may be the right call for the product you want, but it is a deliberate trade and it should be made on purpose rather than by accident.

Cheapest first build with real impact: the morning notification into two doors. Push already exists, so it is mostly copy and one new screen.
