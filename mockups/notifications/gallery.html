The drift intercept escalates. It is a ladder, not a ping.

When someone slips we do not go quiet and we do not wait. About 7 beats over three weeks, each carrying more weight than the last, then a hard stop. Roughly 5 to 6 land inside the first two weeks, which is real presence without becoming Duolingo. Each beat sent once, and the whole thing resets the instant they log anything. The day counts are for a daily goal. A 3 to 4 times a week goal stretches the same ladder later, because a two-day gap is normal, not a slip. This is Maya's channel as the example.

The emotional beat, across every goal

The middle beat is where it gets personal. One strong sentence, no second line forced on, aimed straight at the reason they started. Same headline, six completely different hearts underneath it. This is the one nobody else can send.

These are NOT notifications. They live inside the app.

A notification's only job is to pull someone back to a locked phone. Celebrating to a phone in a pocket is pointless, and coming-back copy makes no sense before they've come back. So these three wait inside the app for the moment they open it:

  • Milestone hit — the target lands. The celebration is on the card when they open it.
  • Comeback welcome — the dimmed card and welcome copy that greet a return after a gap (already built in-app).
  • Consistency landmark — 10, 25, 50, 100 days banked. A quiet moment inside, not a push.
iOS reality: push only reaches an installed PWA (Add to Home Screen). That's why the install push at the start matters so much. Once installed, all of the above is on the table.

Timing is per goal, not per clock. A calorie log fires midday or evening, a morning workout fires at dawn, a sobriety check fires at night. The daily move learns the goal's real time.
