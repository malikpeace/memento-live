The 100

Every candidate component for the Memento page, in ten sets. Four research agents studied retention mechanics, quantified self apps, accumulation interfaces and memorial design; ten designers built from that research; two auditors checked the laws and picked the strongest.

This is a menu, not a design. Nothing here is arranged. Filter by set, or hit The 12 picks to see what the judge rated highest. Every component uses the same persona: day 300, 231 marks, 312 actions, 3 promises, 20,967 days left.

The up only law applies: numbers on this page rise. The one number allowed to fall is the countdown.

Overlaps the judge flagged (31)
  • clk-mirror and mor-clocks: The same object described twice, down to the name: mortality countdown and rising Memento age at identical type size, one ticking live. Keep one, it is the page's thesis and should appear exactly once.
  • vol-clock and idn-beenclock: Both are a live clock counting up from 12 October 2025, 6:42 am, to the second, with the same argument (indexed to a date, not behaviour). Only the label differs.
  • cmb-clock and vol-clock: Same rising creation clock again, with the fourteen gaps printed under it. The gap list is a caption, not a different component.
  • clk-odometer and vol-clock: One duration since creation rendered as a single huge seconds figure versus the same duration as a ticking clock. Same ratchet, same source, different digit grouping.
  • rec-hold and mil-standing: Longest run of 23 days, its exact dates, and how long the record has stood. mil-standing adds one line about what it would take to beat it, which is a sentence, not an idea.
  • clk-standing and rec-board: A table of personal bests with the number of days each has stood. Four rows versus five rows of the same table.
  • vol-stair and cmb-climb: Cumulative marks as a monotonic area with April named as the flat spot. Nearly identical descriptions, only the counted quantity differs (462 marks versus 231 days).
  • amb-stair and cmb-climb: The never-falling cumulative line again, with a missed day drawn as a flat step. Three versions of this chart exist in the inventory.
  • mor-weeks and amb-weeks: The 4,175 week life grid with 1,136 to 1,179 lived, the 43 path weeks in green and the current week lit. Same cell counts, same colouring rule.
  • amb-weeks and ctx-lifeweeks: Weeks of a life as dots with the weeks spent on this lit at the tail. ctx-lifeweeks is the same grid with the unlived half cropped off.
  • mor-strip and amb-horizon: A single life-length bar, lived portion filled, today as a seam, the 300 days as a green sliver just behind it. Identical construction.
  • idn-lifebar and mor-strip: Every day alive as one bar with the 300 recent days lit at the far end. Same scale-honesty argument, same shape.
  • clk-today and mor-oneday: Today as 24 cells with elapsed hours filled, the current hour lit, and the hours remaining counted beside it.
  • clk-units and ctx-ladder: One elapsed count re-divided into a ladder of human units with the arithmetic left auditable. Same move, different rung labels.
  • vol-weight and mor-share: One plain sentence dividing time on the path by time alive, no chart. Weeks in one, days in the other, identical rhetoric and identical purpose.
  • vol-tally and cmb-past: The 140 logged and walked-past distractions rendered as one mark each. Five bar gates versus dots is a mark shape, not a second idea.
  • amb-wall and amb-field: All 300 days at fixed positions, lit in proportion to marks on absolute thresholds, blanks left as dark space. The same heat field twice in the same set.
  • cmb-wall and amb-wall: The 300 day grid again, marked days lit, blanks neutral. The green return days are a highlight layer on the identical object.
  • cmb-gaps and amb-returns: All fourteen away-stretches drawn in order with the day you came back marked on the end of each. Bars versus a cut line, same data and same reading.
  • cmb-line and ctx-silence: The single fact that the worst gap is tiny against the whole record. One states it as a sentence, one draws it to scale, and neither carries anything the other lacks.
  • rec-months and ctx-months: Every month of the record as a bar of marked days with the best and the worst named in place. Same chart, same annotations.
  • rec-rank and ctx-runs: Every finished run of days as a scaled bar with the longest named. ctx-runs adds a median marker, which is one extra line on the same chart.
  • amb-threads and ctx-promises: The three promises drawn at true length on one shared scale, one finished, one set down, one still ticking at 62 of 100.
  • idn-chapters and wrd-promises: The three promises listed with real dates and an honest status including the abandoned one. One quotes the wording, one calls them chapters.
  • vol-ribbon and mil-rungs: A ratchet total on a rung ladder with the passed rungs dated and exactly one next rung named. A track versus a list of the same rungs.
  • mil-500 and mil-oneline: The distance from the current total to the next round number, stated once, no bar. mil-oneline is the same sentence shortened.
  • clk-arrivals and mil-horizon: The next four dated landmarks laid out by distance with the nearest counted down. Same four arrivals, same fresh-start-forward argument.
  • mor-landmarks and clk-arrivals: Four future dates that arrive on their own: day 1,000, the 22nd birthday, the halfway mark. The third instance of this list in the inventory.
  • idn-theroom and wrd-antivision: The anti-vision quoted verbatim with a rebuttal attached. Three dated facts versus a rising clock is a swap of the rebuttal, not a new component.
  • mil-hundred and ctx-pace: The open promise's own established rate projected onto a calendar date for the 100th. mil-hundred adds the 100 cell grid to the same projection.
  • mor-saturdays and mor-summers: Remaining life re-expressed in one small countable unit you can picture, sitting inside a set that already tables both units in mor-remaining.
