From their words to their actions

The machine behind Memento, end to end. Six steps, and the whole thing rests on one principle and two questions.

The principle. A goal is an outcome you do not control, reached through behaviors you do. Memento's entire job is translating one into the other, and showing whichever is honest that day.

The two questions the AI answers, in order: 1. What would count? (classification) 2. What do you control? (decomposition)

The pipeline

What happens between someone typing a sentence and the home screen knowing exactly what to show them.

1

Their words

The person arrives with a sentence in whatever shape their life produced it. Nothing is required of it.

"I want to keep bees."  ·  "Lose weight."  ·  "100 people paying me."
2

The signature

Clarity sharpens the sentence into a star and keeps exactly three things: the goal, a why in their own words (never generated), and the anti-vision, what it costs them if this stays neglected. Nothing else.

3

Classification: what would count?

The AI that just wrote the synthesis names the goal's type. One of six, nothing else is possible:

Quantity upQuantity down FrequencyMaintenance MilestoneOpen

Plus three optional facts: a target if one is named (never forced), a deadline (a date, or a bounding event like "before the baby comes"), and for milestones the hidden engine, the countable thing underneath ("pass the boards" runs on hours studied).

4

Decomposition: what do you control?

The pivot of the whole machine. The outcome is not controllable, so the AI names the input behaviors that cause it. The scale is not controllable; eating under 2,500 is. Getting hired is not; ten applications a week is. Bees are not; building the hive box this weekend is.

Milestones also get stages: becoming a beekeeper is learn, equip, install, tend, and the app should know which act you are in.

5

Actions: verb, size, cadence

Every generated action carries three properties, and each does a different job:

VerbDone meansThe button it needs
Shipthe artifact existstap done
Attemptthe shots were takentap done
Reptime or count servedcounter / timer
Holdthe day ended compliantevening confirm, never a 2pm checkbox
Checkmeasured on scheduleappears on its cadence, weekly is normal

Size is the five tiers that already exist (tiny to extreme). Cadence is how often the action exists at all: most are daily, a Check can be weekly, and that is how the beekeeper gets a seven-day rhythm without the app calling six of those days a failure.

6

The face

The goal's type picks what the home card shows. The action's verb picks the button on it. Quantity goals get the climb and the distance. Frequency gets the rhythm. Maintenance gets the unbroken line and time held. Milestones get the current act and the ledger. Open goals get evidence, their own words accumulating, and never a meter pretending to measure the unmeasurable.

"Is that even a goal?"

The marriage question, answered.

"Don't decide anything about the marriage until January" does classify: maintenance, hold-until January. And maintenance goals do have progress, the same way "stay sober" does. The unbroken line growing is the progress. Day 40 of 150 held is real.

But it is almost never a star. A sentence like that is usually a rule someone holds during a hard season, which makes it an action, a Hold, serving a larger goal they have not named yet. The taxonomy exists so nothing a person types can break the machine. Clarity's job is still to steer them to a star worth chasing, and to quietly file the rules under it.

The boundary test: if it cannot be the point of a year of your life, it is an action, not a star.
