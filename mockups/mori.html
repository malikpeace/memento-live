Memento Mori, 5 ways

The time-left surface. It is the most memorable thing in the app and currently the least visited. These treat it as a reason to act today rather than a poster about death.

01The grid

4,160 weeks

You have used 1,196.

The white one is this week.

What it is. The full 4,160-week life grid, this week marked in white.
Good. Hits harder than anything else in the app on first view, and it costs almost nothing to build.
Bad. Loses force by the tenth visit and needs a birth date, which is a heavy thing to ask for in onboarding.
Seen in The Life in Weeks chart from Wait But Why, and the life-calendar apps built on it.

02One number
2,964

weeks left, roughly

You spent one of them on this goal. Eleven days of it counted.

What it is. One figure, no metaphor, tied straight back to the work.
Good. Survives repeat visits better than the grid because the number moves and the line under it references what you did.
Bad. A number without a picture is easy to scroll past. It has no moment.
Seen in Apple's Screen Time totals, countdown apps, Whoop's recovery score.

03This week, zoomed

Week 1,197

Seven days. Two already gone.

MonSun

Today is the white one. It is the only one you can do anything about.

What it is. Zoomed all the way in: seven squares, today lit.
Good. A week is a scale you can feel, so it works every single day rather than once. The best default face for the module.
Bad. Barely reads as Memento Mori. It is closer to a weekly tracker with a philosophical caption.
Seen in Apple Watch's weekly activity, Duolingo's week strip, Streaks.

04Against the goal

Two clocks

100 paying users 1 / 100
Your life 1,196 / 4,160

At the pace of the last month, you reach 100 users in about 3 years. That is 156 of the weeks above.

What it is. Your goal's clock next to your life's clock, with the arithmetic done out loud.
Good. The only one here that would change behaviour rather than just be felt. The pace calculation is genuinely confronting.
Bad. Needs a believable pace estimate. A wrong 'three years' is worse than saying nothing, and it can read as discouraging.
Seen in Retirement projections, Strava's goal pacing, savings calculators.

05The year

The green stretch is since you set this goal. You have been at it for 36% of the year.

What it is. The year as one bar, with the stretch since you set the goal in green.
Good. A year is a scale people already feel, unlike 4,000 weeks, and it needs no birth date.
Bad. Resets every January, so it is weakest in December and meaningless on day one.
Seen in Year-progress bars, Apple Fitness' yearly rings, Wrapped's timeline.
